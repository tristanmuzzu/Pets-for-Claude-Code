# v0.7.5: one size, and a count that means something

Two things, both about the overlay telling the truth about itself.

## The size control sizes the overlay, not only the pet

Choosing **S** left a small pet under a full-sized card, which is not a smaller
overlay: the cards are what takes up the room. At **L** the opposite — the pet
grew past the gap it was given and stood on the bottom of the card it was
speaking from, because the stack's clearance was a fixed 106px measured for the
middle size.

Everything now scales together. One transform, applied to cards, menu, panel
and hint at once, about the corner the pet stands in — so a card is the same
card at every size: same proportions, same wrapping, same hairlines. The pet
stays outside that transform and is still drawn at its final size, because a
sprite magnified after the fact stops being pixel art.

The window scales with it, or at the largest size its own edge cuts the cards
off. It grows up and to the left only, since that is where the room is, and
never past the edge of the work area — a window that cannot fit gets clamped,
and a clamp fighting the anchor is how a size change ends up walking the pet
down the screen.

That made the stored position ambiguous, because a launch starts at the size in
`tauri.conf.json`: reading back a position saved by a larger window put the pet
a quarter of the way across the screen, again on every launch. A position now
always means *where a window at the middle size would sit under this pet*,
which at that size is the window's own position — so every config written so
far keeps meaning what it meant.

Cards give way rather than clip: their width is capped by the room the window
actually has, so a pet parked near the left edge of the screen gets narrower
cards instead of cards with their left third missing.

The middle size is unchanged, pixel for pixel.

## A plan item is not work in flight

A finished session sat there reading **Finishing · 9 running** with nothing
running, and stayed that way.

The outstanding count is read out of the transcript — an id when work is
launched, the same id quoted back when it ends — and it took `taskId` wherever
that word appeared. Two different things use it: work that was handed off, like
a monitor or a workflow, and the **task list**, whose items are numbered 1, 2,
3. So every `TaskCreate` and every `TaskUpdate` after it registered a plan item
as work in flight. Nothing ever reports a plan item finished, so they
accumulated, and a card could only go green once the whole session had been
silent for five minutes — which a working session never is.

They are told apart by the shape of the id, which is the only signal the file
offers: launched work gets a generated id, and a generated id has letters in
it. A counter never does.

## Verified

- The size, against the built app on an isolated profile: S, M and L with the
  pet's corner fixed to the pixel across all three; a size change from the
  pet's own menu with the pet standing still; three launches and a drag at L
  landing in identical pixels; and the largest size at a left-edge position
  with nothing cut off.
- The count, against the transcript it was reported from, with the shipped code
  and with this one: 9 outstanding before — seven of them plan items — and 2
  after, the two background commands that really were launched and never
  reported back. Then as the app, same session and a transcript of that
  session's own `TaskUpdate` lines: **Finishing · 7 running** before, **Done**
  after.
- 90 Rust tests, 32 JavaScript tests, clippy, rustfmt and the sprite check.

## Still true, and not a bug

A background command that finishes after the last turn of a session has nowhere
to report it: the notification is delivered into a turn, and there is no next
turn to deliver it into. Such a card reads "Finishing · 1 running" until the
five-minute write-off, which is what the write-off is for.
